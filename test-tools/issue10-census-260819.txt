THE QUESTION
------------
issue10-page-identity-260819.txt found the page: one healthy, singly-mapped anon
heap page of sh's arena (user 0x80014000) holding exactly ONE wrong longword,
0x4AFC0000, at frame offset 0xAA0 -- in the UPPER half of the 4 KiB frame.  Two
readings of how it got there:

  H1  a fill left the 2 KiB tail uncleaned: the frame was handed to the anon page
      carrying a prior owner's content in 0x800..0xFFF, and the word is that
      owner's.  Prediction: the upper half is DENSE, foreign, zero-run-free.
  H2  the frame WAS zero-filled correctly and a mis-addressed write dropped the
      one word into it later.  Prediction: the upper half is mostly zero, with the
      word an isolated anomaly sitting in valid arena.

Every named 2 KiB-era tail-zero site is already converted to a full 0x1000 clear
in this kernel -- anon_zero (patch_modelb.py @0xADDFA, "pea 0x800"->"pea 0x1000"),
the block-swap-in klustsize .data initializer and anon_getpage length
(patch_swapin.py), the s5/ufs/spec file-getapage tails (patch_modelb.py +
patch_modelb_pager.py) and segmap_pagecreate's five callers (patch_pagecreate.py).
So H1 for THIS trigger would require a still-unconverted fill site; H2 needs none.

READING IT -- H2, DECISIVELY; H1 REFUTED
----------------------------------------
* The frame was zero-filled correctly.  The longest run of consecutive zero longs
  is 343 (1372 bytes of contiguous zero).  The lower half holds only 101 zeros, so
  that run lies in the UPPER half -- the very region H1 says should be a prior
  owner's dense content.  A stale/under-cleaned page cannot contain a 343-long
  zero run.  80% of the upper half (410/512 longs) is zero.  The demand-zero fill
  did its job; this is not a fill that stopped at 0x800.

* The bad word is an isolated anomaly in valid arena.  Its 64-byte block is sh's
  own parse memory for setup.sh -- the ASCII tokens "if", "[", "-s" (an
  `if [ -s ... ]` under construction) with zero padding between them -- and the
  seven longs immediately after the hit are all zero.  0x4AFC0000 is one wrong
  longword surrounded by sh's own content and clean zero-fill, not one word of a
  page full of somebody else's content.

* Consistent with the page-identity capture and with the fill census above: the
  page's IDENTITY is right (anon, singly mapped, reverse map intact) and its
  CONTENT has exactly one bad longword, in a correctly zero-filled frame.  So the
  word arrived by a WRITE that landed at the wrong address (or a single-longword
  store of a wrong value), not by a page-lifetime or fill-tail defect.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SETTLE
----------------------------------
Settles: H1 (the LEAD's stale-frame / under-cleaned-tail family) is refuted for
this trigger.  The named 2 KiB constants and sites (anon_zero pea 0x800, klustsize
0x800 .data, anon_getpage, s5/ufs/spec getapage tails, segmap_pagecreate) are all
already converted to 0x1000 in this kernel, and this census shows the fill they
govern produces a clean, fully-zeroed frame here -- so none of them is putting the
word in.  These are Model-B 2 KiB->4 KiB conversions the port already carries; the
census confirms they hold at run time for the anon demand-zero path.

Does not settle: the exact instruction that writes 0x4AFC0000 to 0x80014AA0.  That
value is a constant across processes, both of today's boots, and the 2026-07-10
amixadm captures -- a deterministic store, not a race.  Naming its PC needs a
write-watch on that one longword (the store is a single word into an otherwise
clean, valid page, so it need not pass through copyout and cannot be found by a
frame scan): checkpoint 0x80014AA0 across kernel entry/exit and catch the good->bad
transition.  That is the next probe and follows directly from this result.
