THE ARITHMETIC, WHICH IS THE ACTUAL EVIDENCE
---------------------------------------------
Summing the nine modes from the matrix above, with the rounding applied:

    planes  5+5+4+4+6+6+5+5+4                     = 44   == scrfix_planes_free
    bytes   sum of roundup(w*h/8,4096) * depth    = 843776 == scrfix_bytes_free

and without the rounding it would have been **788 480**.  So the measured total can only be
produced by the rounding actually running, and by allocbmap and freebmap rounding to the same
value -- if the two had drifted apart the difference would not be a clean 40960.

    bytes_alloc - bytes_free  = 40960 = exactly the one console plane, never freed
    planes_alloc - planes_free = 1    = the same plane

That is the invariant this unit exists for: a silent divergence between the two roundings
frees a different number of bytes than were allocated and corrupts the chip map, and nothing
else in the system would say so.

WHY PREDICTION 3 WAS NOT READABLE, AND THE INSTRUMENT LESSON
-------------------------------------------------------------
`scrfix_last_raw` and `scrfix_last_size` latch the LAST call.  pokeall's last mode is
640x512x4, whose 40960-byte plane is already a 4 KiB multiple, so the latch shows 40960 ->
40960 and cannot demonstrate the rounding at all.  **A "last value" latch cannot show a
transformation that the last sample does not undergo.**  The totals answered it instead,
which is why the accumulating counters were worth having next to the latches.

WHAT THIS RUN DOES NOT DECIDE
------------------------------
It does not show a user-visible correct picture.  It shows that the plane is aligned and
page-rounded and that the accounting closes; whether Deluxe Paint now draws where it means to
is a hardware question with a display attached.

COST, AND THE COUNTER THAT WATCHES IT
--------------------------------------
Rounding costs at most 4095 bytes per plane: 320x256x6 now needs 73 728 chip bytes instead of
61 440.  Chip RAM is 2 MiB.  A client holding several screens at once could reach exhaustion
sooner than before, and `scrfix_allocfail_n` is the counter that would say so rather than the
failure appearing as an unexplained refused ioctl.
