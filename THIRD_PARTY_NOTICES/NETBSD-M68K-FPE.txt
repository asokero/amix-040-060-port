NetBSD/m68k FPE (floating-point emulation) — copyright notices
==============================================================

Like the two Motorola packages beside this file, the FPE is **extracted at build time**: the 25
files of `usr/src/sys/arch/m68k/fpe/` come out of the pinned NetBSD 9.4 `syssrc.tgz`
(sha256 `5e1f101748d8ff04a37aba845133e0f5804d1ca9b995c99f05cc39874ab3120b`, `config.sh:26-30`)
into `build/fpe-src/`, which is not tracked. No file of theirs is in this repository or in its
history. Nothing in them is edited — not the code, not the licence headers, not the `$NetBSD$`
RCS ids.

**Four machine-ABI headers come out of the same tarball** (2026-08-26), under
`build/fpe-src/include/`, on the same terms:

| Extracted as | From |
|---|---|
| `build/fpe-src/include/m68k/cpuframe.h` | `usr/src/sys/arch/m68k/include/cpuframe.h` |
| `build/fpe-src/include/m68k/fpreg.h` | `usr/src/sys/arch/m68k/include/fpreg.h` |
| `build/fpe-src/include/m68k/ieee.h` | `usr/src/sys/arch/m68k/include/ieee.h` |
| `build/fpe-src/include/sys/ieee754.h` | `usr/src/sys/sys/ieee754.h` |

They are what the emulator's own `#include`s reach for and AMIX has no equivalent of: the trap
and FP frame layouts, the FPCR/FPSR bit names, and the IEEE field widths. Everything else the
package includes is AMIX's own header, which is not a preference but a fact of the toolchain —
the cross-gcc wrapper prepends the sysroot include path ahead of every command-line `-I`, so a
shim cannot shadow an AMIX header even if one wanted it to. `src/extract_fpe.sh` diffs all
twenty-nine files against a fresh extraction on every build, and refuses any file under
`build/fpe-src/` that did not come out of the tarball.

The first-party compatibility headers this port had to write — for the types AMIX spells
differently, and for the two places NetBSD's headers collide with AMIX's — live in
`src/fpe-compat/` and are **not** in the extracted space. None of them is a copy of a NetBSD
header.

This file is here even though the code it describes never is. The obligations below attach to
what a build of this repository **produces**: a kernel with the soft-FPU lane linked in carries
all five families, so the licence surface is real whether or not the sources were ever checked
in — and one of the five has a condition that binds anyone who then advertises the result.

FIVE LICENCE FAMILIES, and they are not interchangeable
-------------------------------------------------------
The directory is not "BSD-licensed" as one thing. It is five notices with materially different
conditions, and one of them still has a live advertising clause. Per-file map, read off the
headers of the files as extracted:

| Family | Clauses | Files |
|---|---|---|
| A. UC Regents / LBL | 3 numbered + an unnumbered acknowledgement paragraph | `fpu_add.c`, `fpu_arith.h`, `fpu_div.c`, `fpu_explode.c`, `fpu_implode.c`, `fpu_mul.c`, `fpu_sqrt.c`, `fpu_subr.c`, `include/m68k/ieee.h`, `include/sys/ieee754.h` |
| B. Ken Nakata, 3-clause | 3 | `fpu_arith_proto.h`, `fpu_exp.c`, `fpu_fmovecr.c`, `fpu_getexp.c`, `fpu_log.c`, `fpu_rem.c`, and the first notice of `fpu_hyperb.c` and `fpu_trig.c` |
| B'. Ken Nakata, 2-clause | 2 | `fpu_fstore.c`, `fpu_int.c` |
| C. Tetsuya Isaki, 2-clause | 2 | `fpu_cordic.c`, and the second notice of `fpu_hyperb.c` and `fpu_trig.c` |
| D. Gordon W. Ross, 4-clause **with a live advertising clause** | 4 | `fpu_calcea.c`, `fpu_emulate.c`, `fpu_emulate.h`, `fpu_fscale.c`, `include/m68k/fpreg.h` |
| E. University of Utah / UC Regents | 3, no advertising clause | `include/m68k/cpuframe.h` |

`README` and `files.fpe` carry a `$NetBSD$` RCS id and no copyright notice of their own.

The two IEEE headers' notices are byte-identical to family A's as reproduced below, differing
only in the trailing `@(#)` line — the same relation the other seven family-A files have to it.
`include/m68k/fpreg.h`'s notice is byte-identical to `fpu_emulate.h`'s, which is family D: it
carries Gordon Ross's live advertising clause, so **the FP register bit names are under it too**
and no build of this emulation avoids that condition by any route.

Family E is new with the 2026-08-26 header import and is the only notice here that is not a
variant of one already present. It adds **the University of Utah** as a copyright holder and is
a plain three-clause BSD: no advertising acknowledgement at all, and a "neither the name of the
University nor the names of its contributors" no-endorsement clause. It is reproduced in full
below like the rest.

Two of these deserve to be called out rather than filed:

* **Family D's clause 4 is not rescinded and is not dead text.** It reads *"All advertising
  materials mentioning features or use of this software must display the following
  acknowledgement: This product includes software developed by Gordon Ross"*. It is a genuine
  BSD-4-clause condition on four files, and one of the four — `fpu_emulate.c` — is the
  emulator's top-level dispatcher, so no build of this emulation can avoid it.
  `fpu_fscale.c` is the case to notice specifically: its copyright line names **Ken Nakata**,
  not Ross, and it still carries the Ross acknowledgement as clause 4. Classifying that file by
  its copyright holder would put it in family B and lose the obligation.
* **Family A's advertising acknowledgement sits outside the numbered conditions.** The numbered
  conditions in these ten files are three, the third being the no-endorsement clause; the LBL
  acknowledgement appears above them as an unnumbered paragraph. The University of California
  rescinded the advertising clause for its BSD-copyright files on 22 July 1999
  (the "License Change" letter, Office of Technology Licensing, UC Berkeley). Whether that
  rescission reaches the LBL wording as it survives here is stated as a reference and not as a
  conclusion; the notice is reproduced below either way, which is what the remaining conditions
  require.

No file in `build/fpe-src/` is a modified version. If one ever becomes one, say so in its own
header and add it to a list here, the way `src/fpsp_glue040.s` does for Motorola's package.
