# F4-M2 — first AMIX boot on real 68LC060 silicon: expectations registered before the boot

This is a **pre-registration**, written and committed before the session it will be judged by.
`AGENTS.md`: *"Write down what you expect before you measure. A prediction that fails is the
useful result; a prediction made afterwards is not a prediction."* Nothing below is to be
rewritten to match an outcome; a miss stays on the page with the reason beside it.

The platform this scores is a **fourth acceptance class**, and its what-it-does-not-model list is
`docs/PLATFORM-LC060-Z3660.md`, committed alongside this file. `ACCEPTANCE.md` §1 requires that
list to exist *before* a result from a new platform is accepted, so the two documents land
together and neither is optional.

Everything registered here is registered for **one clock and one posture** — `cpufreq 50`, the
part's rated speed. The 2× point (100 MHz) is a separately measured experiment and no expectation
in this file transfers to it.

## 1. The preconditions — a dependency checklist, not a wish list

**All ten, verified, or the session does not start.** Seven of them cost minutes; the three that
cost a whole session if skipped are 1, 3 and 10.

> Item 10 was added on 2026-08-25, after the first boot on real silicon reached the banner and
> then panicked in `initialize+0x5C` because the artifact under test carried no driver for the
> disk it was about to root on.  Nothing on the original nine asked that question: item 1 asks
> whether the root *filesystem* can come up clean, item 6 and 7 ask whether the *artifact* is
> pinned and measurable, and §2's rig block named the kernel purely by its FPU content.  The
> gap is recorded in `docs/060-F4-M2-PANIC-260825.md`.

## 3. What "the first boot" means, and in what order

`ACCEPTANCE.md` §11: the cheap and the diagnostic first, the destructive and the slow last. Here
that ordering is also a thermal budget.

1. **Identity and banner** (§4.1) — and the block magics, before any counter.
2. **The counter table** (§4.2) at multiuser, then again after one deliberate user-mode FP event.
3. **`PCR`** (§4.3) and **`CACR`** captured.
4. **The memory shape** (§4.4), which is read off the boot, not configured into it.
5. **The A2 discriminator ladder** (§4.5) — three boots, one variable each, run for the finding
   even though the requester is expected to survive all three. Boots A and C are bare-Kickstart
   boots that cost a config edit and a reboot each; **boot B is the AMIX posture itself**, so it
   is a boot the session takes anyway and the ladder's real cost is two boots.
6. **The driver A/Bs** (§4.6) — arm A first, both drivers, then arm B.
7. **The dhrystone row** (§4.7), last, because it is the only item that is purely a number.

A clean shutdown is part of every step, not an afterthought — see precondition 1.

### 4.1 Banner, identity, `fpu_present`

### 4.2 The counter table

**This table carries the three corrections that `docs/060-F1-M2-GATE-CENSUS-260824.md` §7 makes
binding on this pre-registration.** They are not cosmetic: the naive registration of the first
group would score a correct port as a miss. Quoted from that document, verbatim:

> * **Register the `fpc_*_nofpu_n` counters as `= 0`, and say why.** The naive registration is
>   "> 0, because the LC has no FPU and the gates will refuse". That is wrong, and it would score
>   a HIT on the port as a MISS. The right prediction is 0, with the mechanism named: stock's own
>   `fpu_present` / `prhasfp` gates decline before the bodies are reached.
> * **Register `fpi_ss_skip_n > 0` as the load-bearing signal-delivery counter**, and register
>   `fpc_setup_nofpu_n = 0` beside it as its *expected* companion, so the pair is not read as a
>   contradiction on silicon the way it was on the bench. Ratio to check: `fpi_ss_skip_n` should
>   track signal deliveries, `fpi_ss_pass_n` should be 0.
> * **Register `f60_entry_n == f60_fpudis_n + f60_fline_n` and
>   `f60_fpudis_nofpu_n == f60_fpudis_n`, rather than pinning the split.** The bench put all of
>   it on the FP-disabled arm; silicon may put some or all on the F-line arm. The invariant that
>   matters is that every no-FPU FP event is accounted for and ends in SIGSYS with the machine
>   alive — not which of two equivalent arms counted it.

The table, as it is to be scored:

```text
BLOCK MAGICS -- read FIRST, in this order, at the addresses status-facts.sh generated for THIS
artifact at the load base observed in 4.4.  A mismatch makes every number below it noise.
  fpi_magic  "FPI!"        fpc_magic  "FPC!"        f60_magic  (the 060 glue block)
  kvp_magic  "KVP!"

THE PROBE RAN AND ANSWERED "NO FPU"
  fpi_n                = 1        fpuinit's 060 arm ran exactly once
  fpi_trap_n           = 1        the temporary vector-11 handler fired: the negative probe
  fpi_nofpu_n          = 1
  fpi_badfmt_n         = 0
  fpi_pcr_pre  bit 1   = SET
  fpi_pcr_post bit 1   = SET      EXPECTED, and NOT a failure of our write: a part with no FPU
                                  has nothing to enable.  The probe is the decider, not this
  fpi_savedvec         restored   vector 11 back to its prior entry on every path

SIGNAL DELIVERY -- the WRAPPER counter, and its expected companion.  In SERIES, not parallel
  fpi_ss_skip_n        > 0        THE load-bearing counter; tracks signal deliveries
  fpi_ss_pass_n        = 0
  fpc_setup_nofpu_n    = 0        expected companion: fpu_setup_gated's refusal arm ends in
                                  rts, so the BODY counter cannot fire.  Not a contradiction

THE BODY GATES -- 0 IS THE HIT, AND THE MECHANISM IS NAMED
  fpc_setup_nofpu_n    = 0        stock's own fpu_present / prhasfp gates decline first:
  fpc_save_nofpu_n     = 0        ten of the eleven pinned call sites never reach the bodies,
  fpc_rest_nofpu_n     = 0        the eleventh (sendsig) is the one F1-M2 gated.
                                  A NON-ZERO reading on any of the three is a FINDING -- an
                                  unknown caller, or fpu_present set on a part with no FPU --
                                  and the response is a caller census, not a patch at the body
  fpc_setup_n          = 0        the 060 FP bodies are never entered on this part
  fpc_save_n           = 0
  fpc_rest_n           = 0

THE FP-TRAP ARM -- INVARIANTS, NEVER THE SPLIT
  f60_entry_n == f60_fpudis_n + f60_fline_n            every no-FPU FP event accounted for
  f60_fpudis_nofpu_n == f60_fpudis_n
  f60_entry_n          > 0        after one deliberate user-mode FP event
  outcome              SIGSYS to the offending process, and THE MACHINE STAYS UP
                       (the shell returns; date, df -- without -k -- still work)
  the arm split        NOT REGISTERED.  The bench put all 20 events on the FP-disabled arm;
                       silicon may put some or all on the F-line arm.  Both jmp nullvect,
                       both end in SIGSYS.  Record the split; do not score it

THE SUPERVISOR TRIPWIRE
  kvp_n                > 0        exceptions reached nullvect at all, so the probe is live
  kvp_super_n          = 0        no supervisor-origin FP trap.  Non-zero is a FINDING

IF AN unix-040 IMAGE IS BOOTED ON THIS PLATFORM AT ALL
  every fpi_*, fpc_*, f60_* counter reads 0
  fpi_pcr_pre / fpi_pcr_post = 0xffffffff     the sentinel, never written on an 040
```

### 4.3 The `PCR` read

```text
pcr_boot high word   = $0431 expected      the LC/EC identity, D1's discriminator
pcr_boot bit 1 (DFP) = SET                 consistent with a part that has no FPU
pcr_boot bit 0 (ESS) = 0  expected         nothing sets it: no SetPatch, no 68060.library
                                           (the Mercury's 0x04300601 has ESS set BY SetPatch)
```

`$0431` is a prediction resting on **two implementations agreeing** — an emulator models a no-FPU
060 as `0x0431`, and the card's own extension ROM gates its FPU-disable path on `== $430` and
branches away otherwise — and **not on the manual**. So:

* **`$0430` on a part with no FPU is a FINDING, not a failure.** It would mean the ID field is not
  an FPU discriminator on real silicon, and that two shipped gates (an OS's and a ROM's) are wrong
  for this part. The boot is unaffected: `fpi_nofpu_n = 1` still says what the part is.
* **The `DFP` prediction is equally unsourced, and is registered anyway.** `bit 1 = SET` on a part
  with no FPU is what the bench shows (`0x04310602`) and what an emulator's model produces; no
  manual statement says a real LC060 comes out of reset with `DFP` set. A **clear** bit 1 here is a
  registered **MISS** and a finding about the part — not a failure of the kernel, and not a reason
  to doubt the boot.
* The **vector-11 probe remains the decider** either way —
  `docs/contracts/FPU-TIER1-ENABLE-SPEC.md:238-240` pre-refutes reading the ID instead, and this
  file does not reopen it. The PCR readback is a diagnostic.

`ESS = 0` is registered here because it is load-bearing for §4.7 and for nothing else in the
session. It is **not** a defect to be fixed in this session: writing `PCR` bit 0 is a new kernel
write on a platform taking its first boot, and one variable at a time.

### 4.4 The memory contract — both shapes, pre-registered

The contract could not be settled before the boot: the Fast-RAM window the emulated line uses is
built by a core that does not run in `bootmode CPU`, and the 128 MB Zorro III board seen at first
light is the extension ROM's software `ConfigDev` with `ERTF_MEMLIST` **clear** — an address
range, not usable Fast RAM. So **both shapes are registered, and the boot's own memory banner
decides** — the `Total Unix memory = <bytes>` line the early kernel prints after sizing chip RAM
and building `MAINSTORE`/`VSIZOFMEM` from `bootinfo.memory[]` (`docs/boot-path-map.md:109-111`),
which is a walk of AmigaOS's `MemList` and therefore reports what the *card* actually handed over.

| | shape **M-A** — the aperture answers | shape **M-B** — motherboard only |
|---|---|---|
| `Total Unix memory` | a 128 MiB-class figure | an A4000-motherboard-only figure (single-digit to low-tens MiB) |
| kernel load base | expected `0x08000000` | expected `0x07000000`-class |
| counter addresses | from `status-facts.sh` at that base | **16 MiB lower**, same tool, recomputed |
| what it means | the `$08000000` window is real on the real-CPU path | it is not, and the RAM question becomes a firmware item, not a kernel one |

Registered notes:

* The intended posture pins `cpu_ram YES` for an independent reason — the firmware disables SCSI
  boot outright without it, and says so on the serial. So the posture that gets tested is the one
  that most plausibly produces M-A.
* **The 128 MiB standing rule is an emulated-line rule and is not inherited here.** Whatever this
  boot reports is the real-060 line's RAM shape, re-derived.
* **Do not let this become an assumption a second time.** If the banner is not captured, the
  contract is still undetermined afterwards and this row is unscored — not quietly resolved.

### 4.5 The A2 discriminator ladder — three boots, keyed on the vendor field

The requester of `PLATFORM-LC060-Z3660.md` §2.5 is expected to be an **end-of-chain artefact and
therefore not configuration-removable**. The ladder runs anyway, because it is one config edit per
boot and it settles a question that is otherwise permanently open. **The vendor field is the
discriminator throughout: anything the card itself emits reads 5195, so a ghost that still reads
15163 is off-card.**

| boot | the one variable | pre-registered predictions |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | `ext_kickstart 0`, everything else as at first light | board 2 **disappears** (it is the extension ROM's own `AddConfigDev` and nothing else creates it); board 1 **unchanged** — `Z2 / 15163 / 200 / 4 MB / Bad`; the requester still appears, now listing one board. If board 1 disappears, the extension ROM *is* implicated and the two anomalies are one after all |
| **B** | the intended posture: `scsiboot YES` + `cpu_ram YES` + `ext_kickstart 2` | a **new** board appears — `Z2 / 5195 / 3 / 64 kB` — `OK` if `1:/z3660_scsi.rom` is present with a valid DiagArea, `Bad` if not; **the ghost persists unchanged at 15163 / 4 MB**. If instead the ghost's vendor becomes **5195**, it was the card's own record all along and the off-card reading is wrong. That single field decides it |
| **C** | *only if A and B disagree with the above:* `autoconfig_ram YES` (a 256 MB Zorro III board, product 2) | if the ghost's address or position moves when the **Zorro III** chain gains a member but not when the Zorro II chain does, it is a Zorro III end-of-chain artefact |

**Registered in advance:** if A and B confirm the predictions, the requester is **not removable by
configuration**, and the session's dependency on precondition 2 is permanent until either remote
dismissal is demonstrated or a ROM-side change is made. That is an escalation, not something to
improvise past during a boot.

### 4.6 The driver A/Bs — both drivers, arm A first, opposite signs

The two Z3660 drivers — `z3660scsi` (the piscsi SCSI driver) and `z3660eth` (the ethernet driver)
— carry their cache maintenance behind a runtime gate, and each ships its own full metal protocol
in its own source tree (`docs/BLIZZARD-F3.md`). They are folded in here because they need the same
silicon and the same session, and because **without the precondition-8 pokes only arm A ever
runs**. What is registered below is what makes them scoreable in *this* session.

**Preconditions (per driver, or that driver's run is void):**

```text
z3660_direct_map    == 1      z3660eth_direct_map      == 1
z3660_ci_ok         == 1      z3660eth_ci_ok           == 1
                              z3660eth_sptalloc_unsafe == 0
z3660_dtt0 / z3660_dtt1 / z3660_cacr      captured into the evidence directory
z3660eth_dtt0 / _dtt1 / _cacr             likewise
CACR.DPI (bit 28)   recorded; if SET the run is ANNOTATED, not silently accepted
CACR store buffer (bit 29)  OFF -- one variable at a time; it arms a separate latent hazard
```

**Arm order and the pre-registered signs — deliberately opposite, and that is the point:**

| driver | arm A (`*_cache = 0`, unmaintained) | arm B (`*_cache = 60`) |
|---|---|---|
| **z3660scsi** | **predicted FAIL.** It has CPU-to-CPU aliases the transparent-translation free ride cannot reach. *If arm A passes*, the exposure is not what the analysis says and the maintenance needs re-justifying — not celebrating | 15/15 cycles clean, 8/8 canary binaries byte-identical |
| **z3660eth** | **predicted PASS.** Board memory only, no second virtual alias, so there is nothing for the maintenance to fix. *If arm A fails*, the maintenance is **not** the automatic cure — read `ci_ok`/`dtt0`/`dtt1` and find out which premise was wrong | 15/15 cycles clean, and the maintenance proven harmless and **exercised** |

**Arm A runs first, on both.** A protocol that only ever runs the fixed arm cannot distinguish
"the fix works" from "there was nothing to fix".

**Counter gates, not decoration:**

```text
z3660    : push_n > 0        nest_hits == 0   cq_overflow == 0
           sptalloc_unsafe == 0               bounce_wr_n == 0
z3660eth : push_n > 0 AND inv_n > 0           range_ovf == 0
           sptalloc_unsafe == 0               ci_ok == 1
           40/40 flood ping, 0% loss;  netstat -in: 0 input, 0 output, 0 collisions
```

> **A green run with a zero maintenance counter is a FAILED run**, because it proves nothing was
> exercised.

**And one thing this session may not do quietly:** the 15-minute thermal cap may not fit 15 cycles
in one window. Splitting a run across windows is fine and expected; **switching arms mid-window,
or resuming a cycle count across an arm change, is not.** Each arm's cycles are counted within
that arm.

### 4.7 The first 68060 dhrystone row this project has ever had

No dhrystone number on any 68060 exists in this campaign. This is the first row, and the honest
registration is a **band with a ceiling**, not a point.

**The row is void without three readbacks beside it:** `Clock index` + `PCLK` (the clock is a
table lookup, not the configured number), `pcr_boot` (§4.3 — `ESS`), and `CACR` (store buffer and
branch cache state).

```text
CEILING          45,805 dhry/s  = 916.1 dhry/MHz x 50 MHz    (derived; the ESS=1 reference)
ESS-OFF ANCHOR  ~42,800 dhry/s  = 855.7 dhry/MHz x 50 MHz    (derived; see below)
FLOOR            27,000 dhry/s  ~= 0.6 x ceiling             (CHOSEN, not derived)
BAND             27,000 - 46,000 dhry/s
```

**The derivation, and exactly where it stops being arithmetic:**

* **916.1 dhry/MHz is measured** — a Mercury 68060 at 66 MHz producing 60,423.0 / 60,544.9 /
  60,423.0 dhry/s, spread 0.2 %, with copyback on and, in that document's own words,
  **`ESS=1`** (`docs/060-D-CACHE-KNOBS-PLAN.md:85-91`). The store buffer and branch cache were
  **off** in that run and are off in ours — that is the one axis that is matched, and it is why
  the ladder those knobs belong to climbs *above* this reference rather than toward it.
* **It is a ceiling, not a centre.** The same document records `ESS` as *"on — measured, not set
  by us, inherited from SetPatch"*. This boot runs neither, and nothing else writes `PCR`
  (§4.3), so `ESS = 0` is expected; and the Mercury is a local-bus 060 while this part is served
  from Zynq DDR **behind an FPGA** (`PLATFORM-LC060-Z3660.md` §2.3). Both deltas point the same
  way, and there is no mechanism by which this platform beats the reference at 50 MHz with the
  same knobs off.
* **The ESS-off anchor is derived, and it is the useful one.** The same measurement puts the
  68060's per-clock advantage over the 68040 at only **7.1 %** — 916.1 against the 040's
  **855.7 dhry/MHz** — *with* `ESS` on. If `ESS = 0` costs the whole of that advantage, the row
  lands at the 040's per-clock figure applied at 50 MHz: **855.7 × 50 ≈ 42,800**. That is an
  upper-bounded price for the superscalar bit, taken from this project's own two reference points
  rather than from a rule of thumb.
* **The floor is chosen, and is labelled so.** `assumed`, no measurement on this platform. It
  sits below the ESS-off anchor by enough to absorb the one delta nothing here can price — the
  FPGA-mediated memory window. Inventing a sharper number would be false precision: Dhrystone's
  working set fits the 8K+8K caches, which is simultaneously the argument that the memory system
  is mostly out of the loop *and* the reason this benchmark is a poor proxy for anything with a
  real working set.

**What each outcome means, registered so the reading is not chosen afterwards:**

* **In band** — the row stands, annotated with all three readbacks and with `ESS` stated. It is
  not a comparison to the Mercury unless `ESS` matches on both sides. A row landing near the
  ESS-off anchor is the *expected* shape, not a disappointment.
* **Above 46,000** — check the clock readback and `CACR` **first**. Above the ceiling at 50 MHz
  with the reference knobs off is more likely an instrumentation error than a faster 68060.
* **Below 27,000** — a real finding about the platform, not a disappointment to argue away. The
  candidates, in order: `ESS = 0` (read `PCR`); the FPGA window pricing into even a cache-resident
  benchmark (which is the single largest unstated assumption in the campaign's projection, and
  measuring it is worth more than the row itself); the clock not being what was configured.

Whatever lands, **the value of this row is that it exists and is honestly annotated**, not that it
hits a number. No milestone in this campaign is scored on wall clock except this one, and this one
is scored on being taken correctly.

### 4.8 Boot time

**No wall-clock gate is registered.** Record it; do not score it.

References, so a reading has something to sit against: the synthetic-LC bench rig reaches network
in **18 s** from launch — but that is an emulator with no real disk, no real bus and no requester,
and it is not a prediction for metal. Metal has real seeks, a real bus, and a modal requester
whose dismissal is a human action of unbounded duration.

**One boot-time shape *is* pre-diagnosed**, and registering it is the point: a boot that reaches
the banner, runs `fsck` through all five phases, repairs, and then **reboots in a ~16 s cycle** is
the known dirty-root loop of `PLATFORM-LC060-Z3660.md` §2.1 — precondition 1 failing, not a new
kernel bug. Its signature is `fsck`'s summary line never appearing. Do not root-cause it again.

## 5. Thermal ramp plan

The policy is in force unchanged: baseline with the 060 parked, **WARN** at 60 °C or +30 °C over
baseline, **ABORT** (halt, park, power off) at 70 °C or +35 °C, whichever comes first, **15-minute
caps** on real-CPU runs until a plateau (< 2 °C per 5 min) is characterized.

What is new, and why the caps do not lift at the start of this session:

* the only plateau that exists — **dead flat at 53.52 °C for 45 minutes at 50 MHz with a
  heatsink**, 16.48 °C of margin — was characterized at **bare-Kickstart idle**. An AMIX boot,
  install or driver soak is a heavier load.
* so **the AMIX-load plateau is re-characterized from zero**: 15-minute windows, cool-down and
  baseline re-read between them, and the caps lift only after one window shows < 2 °C per 5 min
  *under AMIX load*.
* **the relative thresholds are the defensible ones.** The absolute °C rest on the shipped
  telemetry calibration and the board's THERM resistor has never been measured.
* the abort clause has fired once already (70.19 °C at T+383 s, un-heatsinked, at the 2× clock).
  It is not a formality.

A run that ends at the cap is **truncated, not failed** — and it is also not a durability result.

## 6. Kill criteria

> **Three attempts without reaching the AMIX console banner and without a named mechanism ⇒ stop,
> write the bug report, and hand it back to F0/F1. This is a bug report, not a re-scope.**

Registered so that "attempt" is not defined after the fact:

* an **attempt** is a boot that reaches the firmware's `060 starting now...` and the Amiga's own
  Kickstart, and then fails to reach the AMIX banner;
* a boot that never reaches `060 starting now...` is a **card/config** failure, not one of the
  three — fix the config or the card and it does not count;
* a boot that **reaches the banner** and dies afterwards is **not** a kill-criterion attempt at
  all. It is a finding, and the response is to name it — the counters and the block magics exist
  for exactly that;
* the dirty-root reboot loop (§4.8) is **not** an attempt. It is precondition 1 failing.

"We think something might be wrong" is not a named mechanism. A miss *with* a named mechanism is
not a kill; it is the next round.

The first two numbers to read on any failure to reach multiuser are `fpi_magic` and `fpi_n`.
`fpi_n = 0` means the failure precedes `fpuinit` and the mechanism is not the one the FPU work
addresses.

## 7. What this session does not establish

Registered in advance, because this is where the next reader finds out which claims they may lean
on.

* **No floating-point result of any kind.** The part has no FPU. Every 68060-only FP item in
  `ACCEPTANCE.md` §6 stays with the Mercury (`PLATFORM-LC060-Z3660.md` §2.1).
* **Nothing about a full 68060 in this socket.** Regimes 1 and 2 are not exercised here at all.
* **No durability result.** The power-cut and long-soak shapes need unattended boots, which the
  modal requester currently prevents, and long runs, which the thermal cap currently prevents.
* **No 100 MHz result, and no clock-scaling claim.** Everything here is registered at `cpufreq 50`.
* **No cure-gate decision.** The ISSUE-10 cure is deliberately gated off on 060
  (`src/hgfault040.s:143-146` — `hg_on`, then `cmpil &40,cputype`), and turning it on is a
  separate, separately measured decision that needs a first-touch-write PoC on **both** CPUs.
  Nothing in this session decides it in either direction.
* **No answer to the soft-FPU engine question.** That is priced by a census, not by a boot.
* **No vector-61 result unless `isp61ea` is actually run** (`PLATFORM-LC060-Z3660.md` §2.8). The
  unit's hardware acceptance is inherited from a full 060 by family argument until then.
* **No coherency claim, in either direction, unless §4.6's arms actually ran** with the
  precondition-8 pokes and non-zero maintenance counters.
* **The memory contract is settled only for the posture actually booted** (§4.4), not for the 2×2.
* **No comparison to any AmigaOS-hosted 060 number** without `PCR` read and stated on both sides
