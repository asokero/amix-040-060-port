## 6. The `ucontext` delta — a new row, n = 1 → 5

`restorecontext` XOR-folds 201 longwords and requires `0xFFFFFFFF`; the printed value **is** the
fold, so the delta from `0xFFFFFFFF` is the XOR of every bit that changed anywhere in the covered
region.  All five samples ever taken:

| when | printed | delta | bits |
|---|---|---|---|
| attempt 4, boot 2 | `0xFFFFCFFF` | `0x00003000` | 12 **and** 13 |
| attempt 5, boot 3b | `0xFFFFEFFF` | `0x00001000` | 12 |
| attempt 5, boot 3b | `0xFFFFEFFF` | `0x00001000` | 12 |
| attempt 5, boot 3b | `0xFFFFEFFF` | `0x00001000` | 12 |
| attempt 5, boot 3b | `0xFFFFDFFF` | `0x00002000` | 13 |

**Every delta lies in bits 12 and 13 and nowhere else, across five samples and two sessions.**
§2.4 registered exactly those two bits in advance, unclaimed, with the arithmetic fit
`0x4008 ^ 0x7008 = 0x3000` — the 68060 format-4 and 68040 format-7 exception-frame words.  What is
**new**: the delta is not a fixed `0x3000`.  Bits 12 and 13 flip **independently**, and bit 12 alone
accounts for three of the four new samples.  On the same arithmetic those are `0x4008 ^ 0x5008` and
`0x4008 ^ 0x6008`, i.e. the format nibble reading 5 or 6 instead of 4.

**No verdict is taken.**  §7 forbids it and the instrument that would settle it — a latch on
`mc_state[0..3]` at `savecontext`'s stamp and again at `restorecontext`'s verify — was deliberately
not built into this artifact.  What the XOR does **not** do is localize *which* longword changed;
that limitation stands, and §7.3 shows it is worse than it looked.

Recorded from §2.4's own reasoning, and it survives: the refusal is **not** a structural layout
defect.  A structural defect fails on delivery 1.  Boot 3b delivered 14,093 signals and refused 4.

## 7. Four static re-derivations, made after the session — three of them overturn a headline

Everything in this section was read out of `build/unix-060-f5uft-CARD1-ced0s1`, the artifact that
actually booted, with `objdump` and its own relocation table.  None of it is a boot result.  It is
placed after the scoring, not inside it.

### 7.1 `nullvect` is the universal trap stub, so attempt 5's Finding 1 headline is wrong — and the real finding is sharper

The session recorded, as its fourth headline claim, *"exceptions that should never reach `nullvect`
are reaching it"*, on the strength of `kvp_last_vec = 32` (TRAP #0, the syscall gate) with 42,400
hits.  That reading is **refuted from the artifact**, and the source of the error is
`src/kvecprobe040.s`'s own header, which describes `nullvect` as *"the catch-all that turns an
unhandled exception into SIGSYS"*.  In this kernel it is nothing of the kind:

* `_start` (`.text 0x0`) does `movec %vbr,%d2` / store to `bugvbr` / `movel #M68Kvec,%d2` /
  `movec %d2,%vbr`.  **VBR is `&M68Kvec`**, set once, and `M68Kvec` has exactly **one** reference in
  the whole image — that one.  Nothing else reads or writes the table by name.
* Of the 256 entries in `M68Kvec` (`.text 0x1378`), **237 point at `nullvect`** — including vector 2
  (access fault) and vector 32 (TRAP #0).  The nineteen that do not are vector 0/1 (SSP and
  `_start`), the six Amiga interrupt vectors `p1int`–`p6int`, `fpsp_vec11`, `fpsp_vec48`–`55`,
  `fpsp_vec60` and `isp61_vec`.
* `nullvect_orig` (`.text 0x11b4`) is not a SIGSYS stub.  It is the shared trap entry:
  `moveml %d0-%fp,%sp@-` / restore `sup_cacr` / `btst #5,%sp@(60)` on the saved SR's high byte →
  `ktraps` (`jsr k_trap`) or `utraps` (`jsr srg_utraps → u_trap`).
* `u_trap` has exactly **one** reference in the image (`0xdbd68`, inside `srg_utraps`);
  `srg_utraps` has exactly **one** (`0x11f0`, inside `utraps`); `k_trap` has exactly **one**
  (`0x11d4`, inside `ktraps`).

So a `trap #0` reaching `nullvect` is **the designed path**, not an anomaly, and
`kvp_n ≈ srg_ut_n` on boot 3b is exactly what that design predicts.  The disassembly of
`libc.so.1` at `0xC10202F4` stands as a fact — it is the stacked return PC after
`moveq #100,%d0; trap #0` — but it names a healthy syscall, not a fault.

**What replaces it is stronger.**  The chain above makes an invariant:

> `M68Kvec[v] → nullvect (the kvp wrapper) → nullvect_orig → utraps → srg_utraps → u_trap`
> is the only route to `u_trap`, and the `kvp` wrapper increments `kvp_n` **before** jumping on.
> Therefore **`kvp_n ≥ srg_ut_n` at every instant**, whenever `kvp_on ≠ 0`.

| boot | `kvp_on` | `kvp_n` | `srg_ut_n` | invariant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **1** | **3** (12:58:02) | **360** (12:57:55), rising to 1270 | **VIOLATED by ≥ 357, and by ≥ 1267 by 13:04:15** |
| 2 | 0-block (userland never entered) | 0 | 0 | vacuous |
| 3b | 1 | 42,400 | 42,392 | holds, `kvp` ahead by 8 |

**On boot 1, a function that can only be reached by passing a counter ran at least 1267 times more
than that counter says it was passed.**  That is a hard arithmetic contradiction measured on metal,
and it is the sharpest thing attempt 5 produced.  Three mechanisms survive it, and this document
declines to choose:

1. **the vector table was rewritten at runtime** so that `M68Kvec[v]` stopped naming the `kvp`
   wrapper (`0x080DA928`) and started naming something downstream of it — `nullvect_orig`
   (`0x080011B4`) would satisfy every other measurement on that boot exactly;
2. **`kvp_n`'s store stopped landing** — a `.data` write that is issued and lost.  `kvp_n` lives at
   `0x0810FF84` and `srg_ut_n` at `0x081106A8`: **different 4 KiB pages**, so a page-scoped write
   loss is arithmetically sufficient;
3. **`srg_ut_n` is being incremented by something other than this edge** — the weakest of the three,
   since the relocation census above shows one call site.

Nothing in attempt 5's artifact can separate them.  Round 6's first instrument exists to.

### 7.2 `kvp_super_n` is structurally dead, so "all user" was never measured

`src/kvecprobe040.s:56-58` reads the saved SR into a register and then tests bit 5:

```text
   da956:  322f 000c      movew %sp@(12),%d1     | d1.w = the saved SR
   da95a:  0801 0005      btst #5,%d1            | <-- the register form: LONG operand, bit 5
```

The 68000-family `BTST #imm,Dn` operates on a **long** with the bit number taken mod 32, so this
tests **SR bit 5** — one of the three always-zero bits between the interrupt mask and the CCR.  The
supervisor bit is **S = bit 13**.  `nullvect_orig` gets this right precisely because it uses the
*memory* form on the SR's high byte (`btst #5,%sp@(60)`, byte operand, bit 5 of the high byte =
bit 13 of the word).

Consequence, measured rather than argued: the branch is never taken, `kvp_super_n` can only ever
read 0, and **every** entry lands in `kvp_user_n`.  Attempt 5's *"42,400 hits, ALL user"* and boot
1's *"3 hits, ALL user"* are therefore artifacts of the instrument, not properties of the kernel.
Boot 1's own numbers show it from the other side: `kvp_last_pc = 0x08000614` is `ustl1`, inside
`userstrlen`, a **kernel** routine reading user space through `MOVES` with `SFC = 1` — a supervisor
fault, counted as user.

Not fixed this round: `kvecprobe040.o` is in the **base** link (`relink-040.sh`), so correcting it
changes every artifact the 030 and 040 arms are compared against.  Registered as a base change and
therefore a separate decision.  `kvp_n` and `kvp_last_vec` are unaffected and stay usable.

### 7.3 `src/srgtrap.s`'s ring reads the wrong stack offsets

The same class of defect, found while re-deriving §7.1's frame arithmetic, and stated here so the
next round does not inherit it.  `srgtrap.s:98-102` documents the stack at `srg_utraps` as

```text
%sp+16 jsr retaddr   %sp+20 pushed USP   %sp+24 frame SR.w   %sp+26 frame PC.l   %sp+30 frame fmt+vec.w
```

The first two are right.  The last three are not: `nullvect_orig` pushed **60 bytes** of registers
(`moveml %d0-%fp,%sp@-`, mask `0xfffe` = `d0-d7/a0-a6`, and its own `btst #5,%sp@(60)` confirms the
count) *before* `utraps` pushed the USP and the `jsr` pushed the return address.  So after
`srg_utraps`' own 16-byte save the frame words are at `%sp+84 / %sp+86 / %sp+90`, and `%sp+24` is
**user `d0`**, not the SR.

Therefore `srt_vec`, `srt_pc` and `srt_d0` do not hold what their names say — `srt_vec` reads the
low half of a saved `d1`, and `srt_d0` reads the probe's own saved `d0`, which by that point holds
`sup_cacr` (`nullvect_orig+0x4` loaded it).  `srt_usp` and `srt_i` are correct.

**No result in this campaign rests on `srt_*`**: the ring was never reported in attempts 3, 4 or 5.
Not fixed this round, for the same base-change reason as §7.2, and round 6's own instrument reads
the frame at the offsets derived here, with three self-checks that would catch it if they are wrong.

### 7.4 Boot 2's EINVAL, symbolized — and the only operand that can vary

`swapconf` (`0x0b401e`) builds its call from the `swapfile` **bootobj** at `.data 0xfe34`, whose
layout is fixed by the code's own relocations (`swapfile+0x9c` for `&bo_vp`, `swapfile+0x90` for the
flags word) and is the classic SVR4 `struct bootobj`: `bo_fstype[16]` at `+0`, `bo_name[128]` at
`+0x10`, `bo_flags` at `+0x90`, `bo_offset` at `+0x94`, `bo_size` at `+0x98`, `bo_vp` at `+0x9c`.

```text
   b4026:  movel #swapfile+0x9c,%d0      | &bo_vp
   b403a:  addil #-140,%d2               | d2 = swapfile+0x10 = bo_name
   b4042:  jsr lookupname                | lookupname(bo_name, 1, 1, 0, &bo_vp)   <- attempts 3/4 died here
   b407c:  lea swapfile+0x90,%a0
   b409c:  jsr swapadd                   | swapadd(bo_vp, bo_offset, bo_size, bo_name)
   b40a6:  tstl %d0 ; beq                | 0 -> done
   b40be:  jsr cmn_err                   | cmn_err(CE_PANIC, LC%10, bo_name, bo_offset, bo_size, d0)
```

`LC%10` reads, from the file: `swapconf - swapadd (%s,%d,%d) failed - errno %d`.  So the printed
triple `(/dev/dsk/ced0s2, 0, 61440)` is `(bo_name, bo_offset, bo_size)`, and **all three are static
`.data` in the image** — read directly out of the booted artifact: `bo_offset = 0x00000000`,
`bo_size = 0x0000F000 = 61440`.  The **fourth** argument, `bo_vp`, is not printed and is the vnode
`lookupname` just filled in.

Inside `swapadd` (`0x0b3138`) the sequence is `VOP_OPEN` → `common_specvp` → `VOP_GETATTR` with
`va_mask = AT_SIZE (0x80)`, and the device size in **bytes** lands in the local the code then treats
as such — proved by the code's own warning string, which prints that local `>> 9` as
`partition size (%d)`.  Four branches reach `moveq #22,%d6`:

| # | at | condition | prints a warning first? |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | `b31c0` | `va_size == 0` | no |
| E2 | `b31c8` | `bo_offset*512 >= va_size` (unsigned) | no |
| E3 | `b31e4` | `(bo_offset+bo_size)*512 > va_size` **and** `swapinfo != 0` | no |
| E4 | `b322e` | after page rounding, end ≤ start, i.e. `va_size < 4096` | no |

and one branch that does **not** fail: `(bo_offset+bo_size)*512 > va_size` with `swapinfo == 0`
prints two lines and clamps —

```text
WARNING: swapadd: configured values for swplo (%d) and nswap (%d) are too large for
partition size (%d). nswap reduced to %d blocks
```

Both strings are in the image (`LC%2` at `.text 0xb30bc`, `LC%3` at `0xb3107`), verbatim above.

**The decisive measurement is a negative one, and the HDMI captures carry it.**  No `WARNING` line
appears on **any** boot: not on boot 2 before the panic, and not on boots 1 or 3b, where the copyright
block is followed directly by nothing (boot 1) or by the four `ucontext` lines (boot 3b).  From that:

* `bo_offset = 0`, so **E2 reduces to E1** and is unreachable separately;
* boots 1 and 3b called `swapadd` with **identical static operands**, did not fail, and did not print
  the clamp warning.  Therefore on those boots `va_size ≥ (0 + 61440) × 512 = 31,457,280` bytes;
* boot 2 failed with EINVAL and printed **no** warning.  Therefore it was E1, E4, or E3-with-a
  -non-null `swapinfo`.

**So the operand that varied between two byte-identical boots is `va_size` — the only operand of
that call which is not a static longword in the image.**  It is a value fetched through
`VOP_GETATTR` on a spec vnode for a slice of a disk this rig reaches through the piscsi driver:
DMA'd metadata, which is the exact class this campaign is in.

Two things ride along, and both are standing findings rather than this round's business:

* the RDB on the work root gives `UNIX_Swap` **18,874,368 bytes = 36,864 blocks of 512**, while the
  kernel's configured `bo_size` is **61,440 blocks = 30 MiB**.  The configured swap is **1.67× the
  slice**.  On boots 1 and 3b the clamp did not fire, so whatever `va_size` reported was at least
  30 MiB — i.e. **the size the kernel is told for `ced0s2` is wrong on every boot**, and only
  happens to be wrong in the permissive direction.  Writing swap past block 36,863 lands in
  `UNIX_Boot`, which on this rig is where the kernel under test is installed.  A durability hazard,
  registered, not exercised;
* `swapinfo` is a COMMON (`.bss`).  If E3 is what fired, `swapinfo` was non-null before the first
  swap area was ever added, which is a second finding in its own right.

### 7.5 The printed checksum is a **second** fold, and nobody has ever seen the first

Also read out of the artifact, and it changes what §6's five samples are evidence *of*.
`restorecontext` (`0x058d76`) does:

```text
   58d96:  jsr stampsum       | fold #1 over 201 longwords at ucp+0xD8  -- THE DECISION
   58da2:  cmpl %d0,%d2 ; beq | == 0xFFFFFFFF -> proceed
   58dc4:  jsr stampsum       | fold #2 over the SAME 201 longwords     -- THE PRINTED VALUE
   58dd6:  jsr printf         | printf(LC%0, fold#2, u+0x8F0)
   58de2:  jsr psignal        | psignal(curproc, 9)
```

`stampsum` (`0x059538`) is a plain XOR fold of `count` longwords.  `savecontext` (`0x058f10`) folds
**200** over `[ucp+0xD8, ucp+0x3F8)` and stores `~sum` at `ucp+0x3F8`; `restorecontext` folds
**201**, which covers the stamp as well, so an intact frame yields `0xFFFFFFFF`.  That much matches
§2.4.

What §2.4 did not know is that the value on the console is **fold #2** — a re-read of the same 804
bytes, microseconds after the read that made the decision.  Nobody has ever seen fold #1.  If the
region is unstable, the two folds need not agree, and every one of §6's five samples is a
measurement of the *later* read only.

Two more facts fall out of the same disassembly, and both are safety arguments for round 6's
instrument rather than findings:

* both `ucp` values are **kernel** addresses.  `setcontext` (`0x04699e`) does `linkw %fp,#-1024` and
  passes `%fp-1024` to `savecontext` and, after a `copyin`, to `restorecontext`; `sendsig`
  (`0x059022`) does `linkw %fp,#-1192` and passes `%fp-1024` to `savecontext` before `copyout`ing
  it.  A probe on either can read the frame without ever touching a user address;
* `u_comm` is at **`u+0x8F0`** in this kernel — `restorecontext` hands `printf` that pointer for the
  `in process: %s` field.  That is a third answer beside `src/usptrap.s`'s `u+0x3b0` and
  `src/sigkill_dbg.s`'s `u+0x1c0`, and it is the one taken from a stock caller's own argument.

`savecontext` has exactly two callers (`setcontext+0x3e`, `sendsig+0x15a`); `restorecontext` has
exactly one (`setcontext+0x9c`).  §2.4's "there is no `sigreturn` symbol in this kernel at all"
stands.

## 8. The ISSUE-10 attribution, re-framed under nondeterminism

Attempt 4 read `User BUS ERROR at 4AFC0008` as a stable frontier and attempt 5's §2.2 connected the
value to `docs/ISSUE10-SETUPSH-WALL-260819.md:119`, where the identical `0x4AFC0000` appears as a
fabricated free-list link in `/bin/sh` on the 68040.  Both readings need adjusting, and they need
adjusting in **opposite directions**.

**What is withdrawn.**  `4AFC0008` has now been seen **exactly once in five metal boots of
cache-armed kernels** (attempt 4 boot 2; attempt 5 boots 1, 2, 3b; attempt 5 boot 3 void).  Attempt
4's "182,741 identical lines" was one boot's screen and is not a property of the system.  Every
inference that treated the *reproducibility of the stop* as evidence for a deterministic mechanism
is withdrawn — including the implicit one in attempt 5's own boot plan, which spent its whole design
on reading a fault it assumed would be there.  **A single-boot signature is now a draw from a
distribution, and this campaign has no measurement of that distribution's shape.**

**What is not withdrawn, and is arguably stronger.**  The *value* survived the loss of stop
determinism.  `0x4AFC00nn` is the m68k `ILLEGAL` opcode word followed by a case count — the exact
byte pattern this compiler emits ahead of a switch dispatch, present twice in `libc.so.1` as literal
`4A FC 00 08` and seven times in `/sbin/init` as the idiom.  Two independent sightings, on two
different CPUs, in two different processes, of the *same text-byte pattern occupying a pointer
slot*, is a fact about **what gets written**, not about when.  A uniformly random corruption does
not land there twice.

**What that does and does not license.**  The ATT5 pre-registration's own rule is kept, not relaxed:
*"Two samples are not a family… It may not be used as evidence for any mechanism unless a third
independent value shares it and an eight-byte `kmem_alloc` header is separately demonstrated."*
n is still 2.

**And the operative attribution is now negative.**  Attempt 4's 68060 fault is **not** attributable
to ISSUE-10 in any working sense, and attempt 5 settles that from three directions:

* the `hg` block read **all zero on all three boots** — the ISSUE-10 cure was never entered;
* §4.1 of the pre-registration shows statically why it could not be: `src/hgfault040.s` is
  `cputype`-40 gated **and** its next screen requires a 68040 format-7 access-error frame, which a
  68060 never stacks.  Ungating it is not a one-line change;
* the 68060 fault was a **read** through a garbage pointer (§2.1's `tstl %a0@(0,%d2:l:8)`), and
  `hgfault040.s`'s own line is *"a read past the break keeps its SIGSEGV"*.

So the two sightings share a **value shape** and nothing else that has been demonstrated.  The
ISSUE-10 cure on the 68040 line is untouched by any of this — it was proven by an install that
completed and a system that reached login — and no decision on the 060 cure gate is taken here.

## 9. `iur_usp = 0x080031EC` — placed, and it is not a finding

The session recorded this as *"a KERNEL-arena-shaped value in a slot named for the USER stack
pointer… the ISSUE-52 CLASS shape"*.  Read against `src/inituser.s`, it is neither a finding nor a
surprise, and the instrument's own pre-registration says so:

* `iur_usp` is written at `ini_user_rte`, the trampoline that builds proc 1's frame and executes the
  drop to user mode.  It reads `%usp` **before proc 1 has ever run in user mode**, so the register
  holds whatever the last `movel <x>,%usp` in the trap-return path left there.  `inituser.s:58-59`
  registers the expectation in as many words: *"`iur_usp` = proc 1's saved USP (**the garbage** that
  becomes a7 after the drop)"*;
* nothing consumes it.  The icode's first instruction is `lea %pc@(L%stack),%sp`, which establishes
  the user stack precisely because USP is undefined at entry;
* `0x080031EC` is `.text + 0x31EC` at the observed load base — kernel text, inside `srvioc`.  Note
  the trap that nearly caught this reading: in an `ld -r` artifact `nm` values are
  **section-relative**, and `.bss` also has a symbol at `0x31e8`.  At runtime `.bss` starts at
  `0x081116F0`, so `0x080031EC` is unambiguously text.

**What it is good for instead.**  It agreed **to the digit** between boots 1 and 3b, as did every
`ini_*` and `iur_*` slot.  A stale register value that reproduces exactly across two boots is a free
**boot-phase determinism witness**: it bounds where the divergence between two byte-identical boots
can begin.  While `iur_usp`, `iur_a7`, `iur_first_pc` and `ini_ret` agree, the two boots were still
bit-for-bit the same machine at the moment proc 1 dropped to user mode, and everything that made
boot 1 a live stall and boot 3b a signal storm happened **after** it.  Registered in that role in
`docs/060-F4-M2-ATT6-PREREG-260825.md` §5, and carried on every future boot for that reason alone.
