   `ucp_sent0_n = 1` on all seven boots whose `ucp` bracket ran (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8).  Boot 6

   fired `ucp_rot_bad_n = 0` over 145,675 restores while the plain fold caught four real events
   (`ucp_bad_n = 4`).  Every scored event is odd-parity; **the scored events are the whole
   population.**  The stock/XOR guard is not missing a hidden class.
3. **The format-nibble bridge is refuted on metal (R5).**  `ucp_fmt_bad_n = 0` over 100,000+
   restores on every Arm I boot.  Every flip is in the dead field, **never** in the format word

4. **The wall was named to one longword and one bit (boot 3).**  The only latch of the session:

5. **The disclosure-zeroing is not the boot cure on silicon (R14).**  All four Arm II boots

   `restorecontext` loop and closed with TCP:23 shut.  `00000000 → 00002000` still breaks the

6. **The emulator reached multiuser with the same Arm II image; metal did not.**  Reported in the
   session (boot 2's scoring): on Amiberry Rig A the `ucz` arm reaches multiuser, on silicon it

## 4. The one latch of the session (boot 3, Arm I, cold) — hex-verbatim

The only boot to latch, and the only one to show the wall.  Work CRC `25A3EE0E` == host template
(f8a1 kernel `efd743c9…`).

**The corruption caught in the act.**

```text
  ucp_stamp   = 'UCF!'   (latched)          ucp_ev_n = 1  (ucpev frozen, 804 bytes)
  ucp_bad_n   = 4        (plain fold caught four corrupt restores)
  ucp_s_bad_n = 0        ucp_miss_n = 0     ucp_self_void = 0
  ucp_b_bits  = 00002000 = BIT 13           (every one of the four diffs a single bit-13 set)
  frozen triple (b-block): index 0x7D = 125,  save 08125734  ->  restore 08127734
                           08125734 XOR 08127734 = 0x2000 = bit 13
  b-block:  b_seq 25477 (0x6385)
            restore  ucp 40001ACC  fold FFFFDFFF (= ~0x2000)
            save     ucp 40001AF0  fold FFFFFFFF
```

`08125734` is a kernel `.data` pointer (`0x0812xxxx`) resident in the dead field `mc_state[125]`.
Only one triple populated (the rest `FFFFFFFF`) → **exactly one longword differed in the frame the
b-block paired.**

**The wall (`putbuf`, the primary console).**

```text
  NOTICE: User BUS ERROR at 4AFC0008, PC:C1027EAA FAULT:6 PID:1 CMD:/sbin/init   (STORM)
  Bad ucontext checksum (0xFFFFDFFF) in process: init
```

The causal chain, visible on metal: `init`'s `ucontext` gains bit 13 in `mc_state[125]` → its
checksum folds to `0xFFFFDFFF` (`= ~0x2000`, exactly `ucp_b_bits`) → `restorecontext` rejects it →
`init` bus-errors at `4AFC0008` / PC `C1027EAA` and storms → never reaches multiuser.  **Same wall
signature as attempt 7 boots 4/5/6**, and this time the bracket named the corrupted longword and
the bit.  Thermal 52.4 °C, rails healthy.

## 5. R5 — the format-nibble bridge is refuted on metal

`…ATT7-RESULTS…` §8.2 (S4) registered a *bridge with a killer*: bits 12–15 of a longword are the
m68k exception-frame format nibble, and `prsetstate` sizes a `bcopy` by `framesz[(mc_state[1] >>
12) & 15]`, with `framesz[5] = framesz[6] = framesz[12] = 0`.  If a flip landed on `mc_state[1]`
and turned a legitimate format-4 nibble into 5, 6 or C, `prsetstate` would copy nothing and resume
`init` on a stale pcb frame — the shape of the `4AFC0008` wall.  `…ATT8-PREREG…` §2 carried it as
the strongest result available to the round, and Arm I's histogram was built to test it directly.

**It is refuted.**  `ucp_fmt_bad_n = 0` over 145,675 restores on boot 3 and over 100,000+ on boots
5 and 7.  The histogram was dominated entirely by format 4 (`ucp_fmt_h4` = every restore,
`ucp_fmt_h0 ≈ 0`), and `ucp_fmt_bad_nib = FFFFFFFF` (never latched).  **Every flip this campaign
has scored is in the dead field, at index 12 or above; not one has landed on `mc_state[1]`.**  So
`prsetstate` is not the writer of anything seen here, and the format-nibble correspondence retires
as a *mechanism*.  What survives is the reason the observation mattered in the first place: the
flips are confined to longword **bits 12–15 by position**, and that position happens to be the
format nibble is now understood to be a coincidence of the bit index, not a fact about frame
formats.  §11 and the electrical assessment turn on that distinction.

**A metal finding rode in on the histogram.**  On the LC060 **every** restore frame is FORMAT 4
(access-error).  The bench LC060 rig (attempt 7 and the f8 bench) saw FORMAT 0.  Recorded as a
platform difference, unexplained; it does not change any R-row verdict.

## 6. R1 — there is no invisible even-parity population

`…ATT7-RESULTS…` §8.3 registered the blind spot: `stampsum` is a plain XOR, and an XOR fold
cannot see an even number of flips of one bit — two longwords flipping the same bit cancel
exactly.  Every event this campaign has ever scored has an *odd* number of differing longwords (3,
1, 3, 1, 1, 1 in attempt 7) **by construction of the detector**, so the population of even-parity
events had never been bounded.  Arm I's rotating fold (`acc = rol(acc,1) ^ word`) is
position-weighted and does not cancel; bench row 4 proved it a genuinely different function.

**On boot 3 it fired zero over 145,675 restores while the plain fold caught four.**  So on this
part, on this path, **there is no hidden even-parity class**: the odd-parity events the plain fold
catches are the whole population, every earlier round's counts are exact rather than lower bounds,
and — importantly for the cure question — the stock guard is not missing most of the phenomenon.
`…ATT8-PREREG…` §6's C2 ("fold only the written region") therefore trades detection for survival
straight, not against an unknown hidden multiplier.  (C2 is still not on the table this round; §8.)

## 7. R6/R7/R8 — value-keyed or address-keyed: inconclusive at the latch, address-leaning in the frame

Two readings exist, at two levels of confidence, and both are reported.

**The hardware-latched pairing (conservative, the scored verdict): INCONCLUSIVE.**  The one triple
the b-block froze carried `V = 08125734`, which was **unique in its frame** (the restore frame held
one copy of `08127734` at index 125, no copy of `08125734`, and no other `V'/V'^0x2000` pair).
With a single copy of V, "all copies flipped" (value-keyed, R6) and "exactly these addresses
flipped" (address-keyed, R7) coincide — this frame did not permit the discrimination.  Scored
INCONCLUSIVE, not forced.

**The full restore-frame analysis (inferential, reported as support): leans address/position-keyed
(R7/R8 shape).**  `verify/boot3-R678-analysis.txt` took the whole 804-byte `ucpev` restore-side
frame — the frame that tripped the latch — and found it to be a **periodic array** (best period 9
longwords, autocorrelation 115/192), i.e. a stale kernel call-chain captured verbatim.  Against
that periodicity it flagged **twelve** longwords carrying an anomalous bit-13 set, clustered in a
contiguous window (offsets `+0x1F0 … +0x264`, plus one outlier at `+0x318`).  For the six flipping
values that appear many times in the frame:

```text
  V=01A10200  22 copies  1 flipped   V=40001ED0  22 copies  1 flipped
  V=40001EE4  45 copies  5 flipped   V=40001EF8  22 copies  2 flipped
  V=40001F70  22 copies  2 flipped   V=40330A00  22 copies  1 flipped
```

**Some copies of an identical value flipped and others stayed clear.**  If the mechanism were
value-keyed, every copy of V would flip; it does not.  So this argues against value-keying and
toward a *position/address*-keyed flip — R7 or R8's shape.

**The honest limit, and it is exactly what round 9 must remove.**  The restore-frame reading
infers each longword's pre-flip value from the frame's own periodicity, **not** from a measured
save-side.  Attempt 8's shadow ring cycled ~47,000× per slot at metal restore rates
(`ucp_save_n ≈ 145k`, four slots), so the true save-side of the failing frame was overwritten many
times over before the frame was restored, and the stamp that keys the ring is not a unique key.
The b-block's "save 08125734" is the only paired save-side the session captured, and it was a
value-unique longword.  **So the address-keyed reading is suggestive, not decisive.**  Capturing
the true paired save-side of the *failing* frame is round 9's first job (`…ATT9-PREREG…` §4.1).

## 8. R14 — the zeroed field does not reach multiuser on silicon

`…ATT8-PREREG…` §6 forbade reading Arm II as a cure and predicted it would not stop the failure.
**Metal confirmed the prediction.**

All four Arm II boots (2, 4, 6-retry, 8) ran the `ucz` arm as built:

```text
  ucp_z_on    = 1
  ucp_z_n     > 0        (44,000 – 206,510 frames zeroed AND re-stamped)
  ucp_s_bad_n = 0        (the re-stamp keeps the zeroed object self-consistent)
  ucp_z_fpu_n = 0        (no-FPU LC060 never trips the FPU gate)
  ucp_z_skip_n= 0        (STRUCTURAL — the LC060 emits only format-0/4, so the format gate is
                          never reached; a silent z_skip is NOT the gate working — registered caveat)
```

The zeroing worked.  And **not one Arm II boot reached multiuser**: each sat in a `restorecontext`
loop (`ucp_save_n` climbing steadily at ~21k/min with no plateau) and closed with TCP:23 shut at
5.5–9 minutes.  `putbuf` on each showed only the kernel banner and the 128 MB memory init.

Two things separate the arms and both matter:

* **No Arm II boot walled and none latched** (`uft_n = 0`, `bad_n = 0`, no `UCF!`).  The
  disclosure-zeroing removes the *visible* checksum-failure symptom — over an all-zero field the
  plain fold usually still passes because a set bit lands on a zero and the re-stamp already
  accounts for zeros — but it does **not** restore boot progress.  `00000000 → 00002000` still
  breaks the fold whenever it happens, exactly as predicted; the guest simply keeps looping.
* **So the zeroing is not the boot cure on silicon.**  Candidate C1 is a disclosure fix and
  nothing more.  The **first-ever 68060 dhrystone row remains owed** — no boot reached multiuser,
  so the pre-registered band was not entered and no PLATFORM readback row was taken.

**And the cross-platform split, reported by the session:** on Amiberry Rig A the same `ucz` image
reaches multiuser; on metal it does not (boot 2's scoring).  The wall is silicon-only.  §11.

## 9. What attempt 8's own instrument could not settle — the post-mortem that scopes round 9

Named as an instrument post-mortem, not a reading.  Three of attempt 8's registered rows were
left open by the *design*, not by the machine, and each names a round-9 requirement.

1. **The paired-save-side pairing failed at metal rates (R6/R7/R8).**  The four-slot stamp-keyed
   ring cannot retain a frame's save-side across a `copyout → user → copyin` round trip that is
   tens of thousands of saves long, and the stamp is not a unique key.  Round 9 needs a save-side
   store keyed on a **durable identity** that survives the round trip and is deep enough to hold
   every outstanding frame — so the true save-side of the *failing* frame survives to be compared.
2. **`uft` does not cover the bus-error fault path (R9/R10).**  `ucp_r_at_wall` read `FFFFFFFF` on
   every boot including boot 3's storm, because the `uft` census (a page-fault/segv hook) never
   latched on the `4AFC0008` bus-error path that the wall actually takes.  So the ordering of the
   first wall against the first restore is still unmeasured.  Round 9 either widens the freeze key
   to the bus-error path or drops the counter as unfireable and says so.
3. **The single latch localizes nothing along the round-trip.**  Boot 3 caught the bit set
   *somewhere* between save and restore, but the instrument reads the value at only two points
   (kernel `savecontext` buffer, kernel `restorecontext` buffer).  It cannot say whether the flip
   is in the `copyout` transit, in the user-memory dwell, or in the `copyin` transit — the
   distinction the electrical assessment now turns on.  Round 9's second arm reads the frame at
   **multiple points** to localize it (`…ATT9-PREREG…` §4.2).

The format-nibble refutation (R5) is a *clean* result and frees the histogram's instrument budget:
round 9 drops the format machinery entirely and spends the room on (1)–(3).

## 11. What this document does and does not say

* **It does not make the software-versus-electrical cut.**  `…ATT6-PREREG…` §5.2 stands.  What
  attempt 8 does is *refute the leading software-mechanism candidates* — the format-nibble bridge
  (R5), the invisible-population/checksum-is-the-bug reading (R1), the disclosure-zeroing cure
  (R14) — and hand a weighed assessment the residue.  That assessment is
  `docs/060-F4-M2-ELECTRICAL-ASSESSMENT-260826.md`; this document supplies its readings, it does
  not pre-empt its verdict.
* **The emulator-reaches-multiuser / metal-walls split is reported, not interpreted here.**  It is
  a strong indicator but not a single-variable comparison (the emulator models neither the
  copyback cache, the FPGA-mediated bus, nor real DDR).  Weighed in the assessment §3.
* **No mechanism for the bit-set.**  A single-bit, always-*set*, bit-position-confined,
  round-trip-surviving flip is a *family of signatures*, not a cause.
* **No cure and no cure PoC.**  C1 is refuted as a cure; C2 is not on the table this round.
* **No dhrystone row, no FP result, no full-68060 result, no durability result, no coherency
  acceptance**, and no driver-source or shipping-default change.  No boot reached multiuser.
